# Model card

Two small fully-convolutional networks, both exported as float32 `.tflite` files with NHWC input/output `[1, N, N, 3]` and values in `[-1, 1]`.

| File | Task | Input | Size | Architecture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `head_g32_256.tflite` | restyles an aligned head crop into a stylized 3D-game look | 256 × 256 RGB | 7.9 MB | `Large(width=32, blocks=6)`: 3 stride-2 convs down to 1/8, 6 residual blocks, nearest-neighbour upsampling with additive skips, tanh |
| `zones_m_160.tflite` | per-pixel zones: R = occluders in front of the face (hands, arms, held objects, clothing), G = hair, B = skin | 160 × 160 RGB | 1.7 MB | `Student(width=32, blocks=5)`: 2 stride-2 convs, 5 residual blocks, upsampling with skips |

The networks use only 3×3 convolutions, ReLU, nearest-neighbour upsampling, additions and tanh, because these ops run reliably on every browser backend that was tested. BatchNorm is fused into the convolutions at export. The PyTorch checkpoints (`head_g32.pt`, `zones_m.pt`) load with `student/model.py`.

## Intended use

- Real-time, on-device, playful restyling of people in a live camera feed (AR filters, art installations, demos).
- A reference implementation and baseline for distilling diffusion image editors into browser-deployable networks.

**Out of scope:** identification or verification of people, any decision about people, inferring attributes such as ethnicity, age or gender, and deceptive media. The model changes faces. Its output does not show what someone really looks like.

## Training data

All training images come from public datasets. **No private photos were used.**

| Set | Pairs (train / val / test) | Source photos |
|---|---|---|
| Faces | 4,323 / 325 / 24 (+36 held-out profiles) | ~3,400 face crops from [COCO](https://cocodataset.org) and [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org) everyday scenes (offices, restaurants, streets, public transport), 997 faces from the [FairFace](https://github.com/joojs/fairface) validation set (CC BY 4.0) chosen to rebalance skin tones and ages, and ~740 profile and three-quarter views from FairFace |
| Zones | ~7,900 images (300 held out) | the same face crops plus ~4,400 scene photos, labelled by MediaPipe's *selfie multiclass* segmenter |

Targets for the face set were generated by [FLUX.2 klein 4B](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B) (Apache 2.0) in 4 steps at 512², using a fixed style prompt plus an automatic per-face description written by Qwen2.5-VL 7B (skin tone, hair colour and style, facial hair, glasses, headwear, view). Pairs were then filtered automatically. A pair was rejected when the face was not found again, when landmarks moved by more than 5 % of the crop, when the scale changed by more than 15 %, when the head tilted by more than 8°, when the image was strongly lightened, or when its coarse structure correlation was below 0.35.

COCO images are distributed under their individual Flickr licenses, and Wikimedia images under their individual Commons licenses (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA). The scripts record per-image credits. Model weights are a statistical summary of many images, not a redistribution of them. If your use depends on the licensing of the training data, check it yourself.

## Training

`student/train.py`, recipe in `student/recipes/head_g32.sh`: 80 epochs (60, then 20 more after the profile views were added), batch 16, Adam (lr 2e-4, betas 0.5/0.999, cosine decay), EMA of weights (0.002). Losses: L1 ×1, VGG16 perceptual ×1, hinge PatchGAN with spectral norm ×0.5 (after 3 warm-up epochs), shift-consistency ×2, plus an extra L1 term around the mouth ×2. Rare cases (beards, bald heads, dark skin, glasses, profiles, open mouths) are oversampled up to ~11×. The augmentations simulate a phone camera: exposure, contrast and noise. Training took about 5.5 hours on an Apple M5 Pro laptop (PyTorch MPS).

Zones model: `student/train_zones.py`, recipe in `student/recipes/zones_m.sh`. Stage 1 trains for 40 epochs with BCE + Dice loss. Stage 2 fine-tunes for 30 epochs, with hands and arms from other images pasted over half of the faces (photos of a hand in front of a face are rare).

## Known limitations and biases

- **Hair colour:** grey and white hair often comes out brown or blond, and bald heads sometimes get hair.
- **Skin tone:** with the descriptions in the prompt, the teacher keeps skin tone much better than without. The student still drifts towards a mid tone on badly lit faces, and very backlit faces can be described (and rendered) darker than they are.
- **Accessories and facial hair:** sunglasses smear, thin frames sometimes disappear, and light stubble is often removed.
- **Pose:** looking down and full profiles are weaker. Faces that MediaPipe does not detect are not processed at all.
- **Teacher bias is inherited:** whatever the diffusion model does to a group of people, the student learns too. The rebalancing reduces this bias. It does not remove it.

## Evaluation

Mostly qualitative: three-column contact sheets (photo | teacher | student) on the held-out test and profile sets, plus on-device measurements ([docs/benchmarks.md](docs/benchmarks.md)). Final validation for the head model: L1 0.24, VGG perceptual 3.0 (in [-1, 1] space at 256²).
